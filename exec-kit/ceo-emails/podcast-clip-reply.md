# Email — The 11pm Podcast Clip Reply

**Use when:** The CEO sends you a link at 11pm on a Wednesday with a one-line message like "Why aren't we doing this?" or "Microsoft says 55% productivity gains, what's our plan?" or "Anthropic just demoed agents writing whole features."

**Do not send if:** The clip is actually about a serious technical or strategic issue that warrants a real response. Use judgment. This template is for the anxiety-driven 11pm message, not for substantive technical questions.

**Tone:** Brief, calm, anchored. Acknowledges the message, doesn't relitigate strategy at midnight, points to a real artifact.

**Length:** Three sentences. Sometimes two. Never more.

**Cadence:** As needed. If it's happening more than once a month, that's a separate conversation about expectations, not a problem this template solves.

---

## Template A — General hype clip

```
Re: [link]

Thanks for sending. The numbers in that clip refer to [specific
context — usually FAANG-internal tooling, a vendor demo on a curated repo,
or a press release without methodology]. Our equivalent figures track
toward those numbers in [realistic timeline — 2027 or 2028 typically],
conditional on the harness investment we started this quarter. I'm
working on the dashboard view that makes that timeline visible — coming
[Friday / next week].

— [name]
```

---

## Template B — Vendor demo / model release clip

```
Re: [link]

Saw it. The demo is real, and we'll evaluate the new capabilities in our
next routing review (in [N] weeks). For our specific stack and tier-2
work, the bottleneck right now is harness completeness, not model
capability. The new model would help us roll out the harness slightly
faster; it doesn't change the timeline for the throughput gains we
committed to.

— [name]
```

---

## Template C — Competitor announcement

```
Re: [link]

Worth noting. Two things: (1) [Competitor]'s announcement describes
[outcome metric], not [process metric] — these usually come with caveats
that don't survive scrutiny. (2) Our own data is tracking to industry-
median improvement, which is real. I'll send a comparison with their
public numbers in our next regular check-in.

— [name]
```

---

## What this template is doing politically

Three things:

1. **Acknowledging the message.** Ignoring an 11pm Slack from the CEO is a worse problem than answering it. The CEO needs to know you saw it.

2. **Anchoring in your existing plan.** The temptation is to write a long defense or to engage with the specifics of the clip. Don't. Anchor in the work you're already doing. The clip is anxiety; your plan is signal.

3. **Pointing to a real artifact.** "Working on the dashboard view that makes that timeline visible — coming Friday." This both demonstrates you're already on it AND gives the CEO something concrete to expect. Make sure you actually deliver on Friday. Once.

## What this template is NOT doing

- Relitigating strategy at midnight. The CEO did not, in fact, propose a strategic change at 11pm. They sent you a podcast clip.
- Promising new outcomes based on the clip's claims.
- Engaging with the specifics of the model/vendor/announcement in detail.
- Defensiveness.

## Variations and pitfalls

**Don't send this template more than once a quarter to the same CEO.** If the 11pm clips keep coming, the underlying issue isn't the clips. Schedule a conversation about how to set expectations between you. That conversation is its own thing — see Chapter 52 of the book.

**Don't send any template if the CEO's question is genuinely strategic.** "Should we be evaluating Cursor for the platform team?" is not an anxiety message; it's a real question that deserves a real response (probably the next morning, after sleep).

**Don't be cute.** No emoji, no humor, no apologies for the late hour, no "in case you missed it." The CEO's mood at 11pm is anxious; your job is to project competence and have it.

## Why three sentences

The CEO is in bed reading their phone. Anything longer than three sentences either gets skimmed or amplifies the anxiety. The whole point of the reply is to lower the temperature, not raise it.

## When to escalate to a longer conversation

If the same theme comes up three times in a month — same clip, same competitor, same vendor — you have a CEO-anxiety pattern, not a series of one-off messages. The right move is a calendar block: "Want to do a 30-min walkthrough of where we are on AI tooling vs the market this Friday?" That conversation should be in person or on video, not over email or Slack.
