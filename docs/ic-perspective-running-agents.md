# The IC Perspective — Running Multiple Agents (Ch 43.6)

> Companion to *Software Engineering with AI*, Chapter 43.6. This is the experiential,
> individual-contributor counterpart to the organizational material in Chapter 43 (Human
> Skill Development). It's mostly lived-experience prose in the book; this page captures
> the durable, actionable parts for the repo.

Chapter 43.6 is largely narrative and lives in full in the book. The points worth keeping
operationally close:

- **It's a cognitive shift, not just a speed-up.** Running several agents is more like
  reviewing and dispatching than typing. The scarce resource becomes *your attention as a
  reviewer*, not your typing speed — which is why the seven slop signatures (Ch 2) and the
  reviewer discipline (Ch 22) are the load-bearing skills.
- **Know when to stop.** There are tasks where spinning up an agent is slower than just
  typing the code — small, local, well-understood changes where writing a good
  agent-ready issue costs more than doing the work. Going back to typing is a legitimate
  choice, not a failure.
- **The skill nobody warns you about** is context-switching cost across parallel agents:
  holding several half-finished tasks in your head degrades review quality. Cap your
  concurrency at what you can actually review well.
- **The morale piece is real.** The shift changes what the work *feels* like; name it on
  the team rather than pretending the day-to-day is unchanged.
