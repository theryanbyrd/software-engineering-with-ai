# Platform Engineering Manager

## At [Company]

[Company description.]

## The role

You'll lead our platform team — a real product team whose customers are the rest of our engineering organization. The team builds, ships, and maintains the harness that makes AI-native engineering work for stream-aligned teams.

This is engineering management, with the discipline of product management overlaid. You'll run a roadmap, talk to customers (stream-aligned teams), prioritize ruthlessly, and own the team's outcomes — not just its capacity.

The platform team is one of the most leveraged in the org. The work shapes how the rest of engineering operates. You'll partner with stream-aligned EMs, with security, with SRE, with engineering leadership; you'll occasionally push back on senior leaders when scope is being eroded.

## What you'll do

- **Run the roadmap.** Quarterly planning. Backlog prioritization. Hard tradeoffs about what's in and what's out.
- **Talk to customers.** Quarterly user interviews with stream-aligned teams. Office hours. Adoption metrics. Their satisfaction is your team's success metric.
- **Manage the team.** 1:1s with each engineer. Career development. Performance management. Hiring.
- **Partner with engineering leadership.** Quarterly roadmap reviews. Budget conversations. Strategic alignment with the broader engineering direction.
- **Defend scope.** Stream-aligned teams will ask the platform team to do their feature work; you say no constructively. Senior leaders will ask the platform team to expand into adjacent areas; you advocate for focus.
- **Make hiring real.** Senior engineers join because the work is real and the team is well-led. You're the person who makes that true.

## What you'll NOT do

- Code primarily. This is an EM role; you'll review some, contribute occasionally on harness components, but the primary work is leadership and product.
- Run on-call for stream-aligned services. The platform team's on-call is limited to our own systems.
- Be the architect. Senior platform engineers (L5/L6) are the architects; you partner with them, you don't compete.
- Be the tech lead. Same — senior ICs lead technically; you lead organizationally.

## What we're looking for

### Required

- **Engineering background.** You've been an engineer; you've shipped production code in your career. You can read code; you can hold a substantive conversation with senior engineers; you don't need to write code daily but you understand it.
- **Product management experience or instincts.** You've run a roadmap. You've made hard prioritization calls. You've talked to users. If you've never managed product, you've at least operated as a TPM or strong tech lead.
- **People management track record.** You've managed engineers; you've grown them; you've made hard performance calls. Specific stories.
- **AI tooling fluency.** Personal use, comfort with the tools, opinions backed by experience. You don't need to be a power user but you can't be a stranger.
- **Communication.** You write a lot. The writing is good — clear, tight, honest about tradeoffs.
- **Senior-engineer credibility.** Senior platform engineers will respect your judgment; you'll respect theirs. You navigate the senior IC / EM tension well.

### Nice-to-have

- Prior platform / DevEx / internal-tools team management
- Background as a senior IC before transitioning to management
- Public technical writing or speaking
- Experience as a TPM or strong tech lead before transitioning to EM

### What we're NOT looking for

- EMs from non-engineering backgrounds. The role requires engineering depth.
- "Tech leads who got promoted to manage." If you don't enjoy the people-management side, this isn't your role.
- Empire-builders. The platform team should be small (6-12 engineers); we don't want growth for growth's sake.
- EMs without product instincts. We're not hiring for "manage capacity"; we're hiring for "ship a product team."

## How we interview

5 rounds, ~6 hours.

1. **Hiring manager screen** (45 min). Why platform EM specifically; your career arc; high-level fit.
2. **Roadmap exercise** (~2 hours async). We give you a fictional version of our team's current state. You produce a quarterly roadmap with prioritization reasoning.
3. **People-management deep-dive** (60 min live). We work through 2-3 specific people-management scenarios — calibration, performance, growth, retention. We grade judgment.
4. **Stakeholder navigation** (60 min live). A scenario involving competing priorities — stream-aligned EMs want different things from your team; security has a regulatory ask; the CTO wants something else. You navigate. We grade structure and communication.
5. **Reference and final fit** (60 min). Senior engineers from the team plus an engineering leadership peer.

## What success looks like at 12 months

A year in:
- The team has shipped an adoption-validated harness
- Stream-aligned teams have stopped asking the platform team to do their feature work (because the boundary is clear and respected)
- The team's senior engineers are doing the most leveraged work of their careers and aren't looking elsewhere
- Engineering leadership has visible, calibrated metrics on the platform team's impact
- Hiring has filled critical gaps; the bench is strong
- Boundary disputes have decreased over the year as the discipline has set in

## Apply if

- You enjoy the people-management side of engineering leadership
- The harness is the work you find most leveraged in 2026
- You've been a strong IC before; you're not running from coding, you're moving toward leverage
- You want to shape how an entire engineering org operates

## Don't apply if

- You think EM is the path to "less coding" (it is, but for the wrong reasons)
- You're empire-building (the platform team should be small)
- You don't enjoy hard product prioritization conversations
