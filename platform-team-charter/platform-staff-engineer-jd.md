# Senior Platform Engineer

## At [Company]

[Company description.]

## The role

You'll be a senior IC on our platform team. The harness is real product work, and at this level, you own significant pieces of it — driving the roadmap, mentoring less senior platform engineers, partnering with stream-aligned team leads on the highest-leverage problems.

The platform team is small (typically 6-12 engineers) and high-leverage. At the senior IC level, you'll be one of 2-4 engineers who anchor the team. Your work shapes how the rest of the engineering organization operates.

## What you'll do

- **Own a major surface of the harness.** Skills library, hooks platform, subagent infrastructure, MCP gateway — pick the surface that matches your strengths. You'll be the named owner; others on the team will work with you on it.
- **Drive the roadmap.** Quarterly priorities. Backlog grooming. The hard tradeoffs about what's in and what's out.
- **Mentor.** Pair-driving with newer platform engineers. Code review with calibration discipline. Teaching the seven slop signatures (Ch 22 §22.2) to anyone who needs it.
- **Partner with engineering leadership.** When the CTO or VP needs to make a decision involving AI tooling, infrastructure, or harness, you're in the conversation. Sometimes you write the memo.
- **Cross-team navigation.** Stream-aligned teams have requests; some are scope-aligned, some are not. You're the senior voice that says no constructively, finds the right path, and maintains the platform team's product focus.
- **Own the hard problems.** When the harness has a deep architectural issue — a hook that's producing too many false positives, a subagent that's too slow at scale, an MCP server that's wrong about its threading model — you're the engineer who works on it.

## What we're looking for

### Required

- **6+ years of engineering experience.** Including significant work as a senior IC. Stories about owning major systems, navigating architectural decisions, mentoring engineers.
- **Demonstrable harness work.** Open source, internal-tools, developer-platform — something where you've built tools that other engineers used. Specific stories.
- **Architectural depth.** Per Ch 5 §5.2, the Architecture discipline. You know how to design systems that resist drift, that fail well under stress, that other engineers can extend without breaking.
- **AI tooling depth.** You've gone deep on at least one major AI tool. You can articulate the failure modes, the workflow patterns that work, the harness investments that pay off.
- **Strong opinions, lightly held.** You'll disagree with stream-aligned teams about scope. You'll disagree with vendor reps about features. You'll occasionally disagree with engineering leadership about strategy. You hold the conversation well; you change your mind when the data warrants.
- **Mentorship track record.** Engineers who've worked with you have shipped better work because of it. Specific stories.

### Nice-to-have

- Prior staff / principal IC role at a comparable company
- Notable open-source contributions
- Background in compilers, languages, programming systems, or developer tooling
- Experience with multiple AI tools (deep familiarity, not just usage)
- Public technical writing or speaking

### What we're NOT looking for

- Engineers wanting to "manage" without managing. We have a real EM role; senior IC is its own thing.
- Architects without recent shipping experience. The platform team ships continuously; design without shipping is not the work.
- Senior engineers who haven't engaged with AI tooling deeply. The harness can't be designed by someone who hasn't used the tools at depth.

## What you can expect

- **Ownership.** A major surface of the platform is yours. You set the roadmap; you're accountable for outcomes.
- **Influence.** Your work shapes how 50-200 engineers in stream-aligned teams operate. The leverage is real.
- **Senior peers.** 1-3 other senior platform engineers on the team. You'll be peers, not solo.
- **Compensation.** L5/L6 IC bands at this company. Equity weighted heavily. The platform team's seniors are paid like their stream-aligned counterparts.
- **Career path through L6.** Principal Platform Engineer is real; we have one (or are hiring for it). The IC ceiling is not L4.

## How we interview

The same 5-round structure as the L4 role, with calibration to senior level:

1. **Hiring manager screen.** More substantive at this level — we want to understand your career arc, your senior IC stories, the kind of platform work you're drawn to.
2. **Code review with AI tooling.** Senior calibration; we pay attention to the depth of your reasoning, not just whether you spot the issues.
3. **Harness component design.** Senior calibration; we want architectural reasoning, not just a working component.
4. **Strategic conversation.** Substituted for the customer-empathy round at this level. We discuss a real strategic question we've been working on; you engage as a senior IC peer would.
5. **Reference and final fit.** Two senior engineers from the team plus one engineering leadership member.

## Apply if

- You've been a senior IC and want to keep being one — not transition to management
- The harness is the work you find most leveraged in 2026
- You enjoy mentoring and pair-driving as part of the work, not as a side responsibility
- You want to shape how an entire engineering org operates, without wanting to manage it
