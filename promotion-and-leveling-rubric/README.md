# Promotion and Leveling Rubric — IC Track

The explicit L3-L7 IC ladder for the AI-native engineering era. Direct implementation of Chapter 42 and Chapter 5 §5.2 of [_Software Engineering with AI: A Practical Handbook for the Claude Code Era_](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ryanbyrd) by Ryan Byrd.

The framing from Ch 42 §42.1:

> The properties that separate engineers who get 30%+ throughput gains from AI tooling from engineers who get 0% or who get slower:
> - Strong code-review intuition
> - Specification clarity
> - System-level reasoning
> - Tooling fluency
> - Skepticism without cynicism

This rubric encodes those properties as level-by-level expectations. It pairs with `junior-trajectory/` (which handles L3 development) and `solutions-engineer-and-tech-pm/` (which converges adjacent roles into the ladder).

## The book's stance

Two chapters anchor this:

**Ch 5 §5.2** introduces the three bottleneck disciplines:

> The three new bottleneck disciplines: Direction, Architecture, Evaluation [...] hire for depth in one of the three, with credible competence in the other two.

**Ch 42 §42.1** describes the high-value engineer profile in 2026 — code review intuition, spec clarity, system reasoning, tooling fluency, calibrated skepticism.

The rubric synthesizes these. Direction / Architecture / Evaluation are the seniority disciplines. The five high-value properties are the level-progression dimensions. Together, they produce a ladder that recognizes the work that actually matters in 2026, rather than the pre-AI work that some ladders still reward.

## What this rubric WILL do

- Establish consistent expectations at each level across the org
- Surface the new work (harness contribution, code review judgment, AI-tooling fluency) that some legacy ladders fail to recognize
- Provide a structured conversation for promotion (and for "you're not ready yet")
- Give calibration committees the language to handle AI-tooling work consistently

## What this rubric will NOT do

- Will not eliminate disagreements. Calibration is hard; the rubric reduces but doesn't eliminate friction.
- Will not work without leadership commitment to the criteria. If the head of engineering is willing to override the rubric for political reasons, the rubric is decorative.
- Will not work as a one-time exercise. The rubric needs annual review as AI tooling and the engineering work evolves.
- Will not transfer 1:1 to other companies. Levels (L3 vs L4 etc.) and titles vary; adapt to your specific structure.

## The core idea

Three things change at each level:

1. **Scope.** What problems the engineer owns end-to-end.
2. **Direction / Architecture / Evaluation depth.** Per Ch 5 §5.2, depth in one discipline with competence in others; the depth requirement increases with level.
3. **AI tooling fluency.** From "uses skills" (L3) to "shapes the org's tooling stack" (L7).

The other dimensions (code review judgment, spec clarity, system reasoning, calibrated skepticism, harness contribution) are present at every level, weighted differently as you go up.
