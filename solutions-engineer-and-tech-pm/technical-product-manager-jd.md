# Technical Product Manager — Job Description (AI-Native Era)

A template for a Technical PM JD calibrated to Ch 42 §42.5's framing:

> Tech PMs increasingly write executable specs (Chapter 4), workshop them with the Intake pattern (Chapter 19), and ship features with light engineering involvement on bounded T1/T2 work. The line between PM and engineer blurs in the other direction.

## How to use this template

This is a **technical** PM role. It is not a "PM with a technical opinion." If you're hiring for a non-technical PM role, this template is wrong; use a more standard PM JD.

The bar this JD encodes excludes a substantial fraction of the historical PM candidate pool. Expect a smaller, more expensive search; expect higher signal in the candidates you do find.

# Technical Product Manager

## At [Company]

[Company description.]

## The role

You'll own a product area end-to-end. The discovery, the strategy, the prioritization, the coordination — the full PM job. What's different in 2026: you'll also write executable specs that engineering teams hand to AI agents and ship from. You'll workshop those specs with engineers and customers using the Intake pattern (or your team's equivalent). On bounded T1/T2 work, you'll occasionally ship the change yourself, with engineering review.

This is a technical PM role for the AI-native era. We expect you to read code, hold a cost-vs-capability conversation with engineering, and recognize when an AI-generated solution is shipping the wrong feature confidently. We don't expect you to be a senior engineer; we do expect you to be technical enough to do the job we're describing.

## What you'll do

- **Discovery and strategy.** Standard PM work. Talk to customers; identify the real problem; build a thesis; defend the thesis with evidence.
- **Roadmap and prioritization.** Standard PM work. The hard tradeoffs about what's in and what's out. The negotiations with engineering and design.
- **Executable specs.** This is the part that distinguishes the role. You write specs that an engineer can hand to an agent and ship. The spec is concrete enough that it does not require a 40-minute "what does this mean" call before implementation.
- **Spec workshops.** You run cross-functional workshops where the spec is refined live with engineering and design. The Intake pattern (Ch 19) is one approach; pick what works for your team.
- **Cost-vs-capability conversations.** When engineering says "this would cost N tokens per run at scale," you can hold the conversation. You understand what the costs are, what the trade-offs are, and you can make decisions calibrated to those costs.
- **Light engineering on T1 work.** When a feature is small and well-understood, you sometimes ship the change yourself. The engineer reviews. You ship. This is occasional, not routine — but it's the muscle that keeps your specs grounded.
- **Cross-functional partnership.** You partner with engineering managers, designers, customer success, and sales. You translate engineering's reality to the rest of the org and the rest of the org's reality to engineering.

## What you'll NOT do

- Write detailed engineering tickets that engineers ignore and rewrite. (If your specs are getting ignored, the spec is the problem.)
- Make commitments to customers about delivery dates without engineering confirmation.
- Run roadmaps from a Gantt chart that's untethered from how the team actually works.
- Pretend you're an engineer when the work is genuinely engineering. (Light engineering on T1 work means light engineering, not the senior engineer's job.)

## What we're looking for

### Required

- **Technical background.** A degree in CS, ECE, or equivalent; OR engineering experience earlier in your career (3+ years writing production code); OR strong technical work as a TPM at a previous company.
- **Executable spec experience.** You can write a spec that an engineer reads in 10 minutes and understands what to ship. We will test this in the interview.
- **Cost-vs-capability fluency.** You understand what AI-tooling costs are and how to reason about them. We will test this in the interview.
- **Customer empathy.** You can hold a customer conversation that surfaces what they actually need vs. what they're asking for. You write notes from the conversation that are usable.
- **Communication discipline.** You write a lot. PR descriptions, design docs, status updates, executive memos. The writing is good — clear, tight, no jargon.

### Nice-to-have

- Experience writing executable specs at a previous AI-native company
- Open-source contributions or technical writing
- Experience as an engineer at the senior level (L4+) before transitioning to PM
- Experience working with design systems (this matters for product surface)

### What we're NOT looking for

- "I'm a great PM but not technical." This is a technical PM role. The technical bar is real.
- Spec-as-Jira-ticket experience. We're not hiring for "writes user stories that engineering breaks down further."
- Product strategy without execution. The role includes shipping specs that ship features, not only thinking about features.

## How we interview

Per [`tpm-interview-rubric.md`](tpm-interview-rubric.md):

1. **Hiring manager screen** (45 min). Why this role; your technical background; high-level fit.
2. **Executable spec exercise** (~2 hours async). We give you a customer problem and a codebase summary. You write the spec. We grade specifically on whether the spec is agent-ready.
3. **Cost-vs-capability conversation** (60 min). Live, with one of our engineering managers. A real-shaped problem with cost trade-offs. We grade on the structure and substance of the conversation.
4. **Cross-functional simulation** (60 min). A scenario involving competing priorities, a mock customer, a mock engineer, a mock designer. We grade on how you navigate.
5. **Reference and final fit** (60 min). Two senior engineers and one designer from the team you'd partner with.

The interview load is ~5 hours of real work plus screens. We do not run case studies or product-sense-without-context interviews; those filter for the wrong things.

## What this role is NOT becoming

We don't think TPM merges into engineering in 2026. The product-strategy and customer-discovery dimensions are real and persistent. We do think the bar continues to rise on the engineering side; we'll keep the JD calibrated as it does.

The career path includes options to move into engineering management, product leadership, or staying as IC TPM through a Principal TPM level. See [`career-path-and-comp.md`](career-path-and-comp.md).

## Apply if

- You're a technical person who's chosen PM as your craft (and shipped real PM work, not just had opinions)
- You write specs that engineers actually use
- You can hold a cost-vs-capability conversation with engineering without bluffing
- You're tired of being underestimated as "just a PM" because the bar at most companies is low

## Don't apply if

- You're a non-technical PM looking for a more technical role to grow into. Possible at other companies; not this one.
- You think AI tooling will mostly automate engineering and you don't need to engage with it
- You don't enjoy writing
- You'd rather not have your specs evaluated against a public bar
