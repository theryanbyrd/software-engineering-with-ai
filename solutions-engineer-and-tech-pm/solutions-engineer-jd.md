A template for a Solutions Engineer JD calibrated to Ch 42 §42.5's framing:

> Solutions Engineers (in customer-facing teams) increasingly write working code in customer environments. The line between SE and engineer blurs. They need real engineering skills, not just product knowledge.

# Solutions Engineer

## At [Company]

[Company description — 2-3 sentences. What we build, what we sell, who we sell to, why we matter.]

## The role

You'll be the senior technical voice in customer conversations. The customer hands you a problem; you hand them a working integration in their environment, an executable spec their team can ship from, or a verified-working POC that shortens their evaluation by weeks.

This is a Solutions Engineer role for the AI-native era. You will write code in customer environments — sometimes in their codebase, sometimes adjacent. You'll use AI tooling productively in those environments. You'll need real engineering judgment, not product knowledge memorized from slides.

We are not hiring you to do demos. We are hiring you to write working code in customer contexts that engineers in those companies are willing to take a PR review on.

## What you'll do

- **Pre-sales technical work.** Discovery calls with the customer's engineering and architecture leads. Identify what they actually need vs. what the salesperson heard. Translate to specific scope.
- **POCs and integrations.** Write working code — sometimes in our SDK, sometimes in their codebase, sometimes glue between systems. The code is real, gets reviewed by real engineers, and ships. Not toy demos.
- **Workshop the spec.** Run executable-spec workshops with the customer's team using the [Tixie pattern](../../skills/) or your team's equivalent. The output is a spec the customer's engineers can hand to an agent and ship.
- **Co-design with our engineering.** When the customer needs a feature we don't have, you're the bridge. You translate the customer's domain into the language of our engineering team's roadmap. Your specs land in our backlog when warranted.
- **Hand off cleanly.** Once the customer's team is ramped, you hand off to Customer Success. The handoff is documentation the CS team can act on, not "you'll figure it out."

## What you'll NOT do

- Run scripted demos that don't touch the customer's environment.
- Build slides that explain how it would work without showing it work.
- Spend 80% of your time in CRM. (15-25% is realistic; 80% is a sales role with a technical title.)
- Resolve ongoing production incidents. (That's CS / support; this role hands off after integration.)

## What we're looking for

### Required

- **Real engineering background.** You've shipped production code in your prior career. Not "I built a startup MVP"; production code, with code reviews from people you respect, with bugs you fixed.
- **AI tooling fluency.** You can run a Claude Code or Cursor session productively. You can write an agent-ready spec. You can recognize when an AI-generated solution is wrong and articulate why.
- **Customer-environment comfort.** You can drop into a codebase you've never seen, read it, and write code in it that fits the conventions. You're not paralyzed by unfamiliar stacks.
- **Specific communication.** You can run a 30-minute discovery call that produces a written summary of what the customer actually needs. The customer's engineering team reads the summary and recognizes their own situation.
- **Sales partnership.** You work with salespeople without being one. You support the deal without overcommitting our roadmap.

### Nice-to-have

- Experience in our specific industry or with our specific customer profile
- Open-source contributions or technical writing
- Experience as an actual engineer at the senior level (L4+) in a previous role
- Experience as a Solutions Architect at a public cloud or major SaaS company

### What we're NOT looking for

- Demo-only experience. If your prior SE role was 90% scripted demos, you'll struggle here.
- Pure product knowledge. We can teach you our product; we can't teach you engineering judgment in 6 months.
- "I'm transitioning from sales to technical." Possible but the curve is steep; we typically don't hire for this.

## How we interview

The interview process tests whether you can do the work, not whether you can talk about it. See [`se-interview-rubric.md`](se-interview-rubric.md) for the structure.

The rounds:

1. **Hiring manager screen** (45 min). Why this role; your engineering background; high-level fit.
2. **Customer-environment coding exercise** (~3 hours, mostly async). We give you a sanitized customer-like codebase and a discovery summary. You design and implement an integration. We grade what you ship and how you got there.
3. **Spec workshop simulation** (60 min). You run a "discovery + scoping" conversation with one of our engineers playing a customer. We grade specifically: did the spec that came out of it become agent-ready?
4. **System design with AI tooling in the room** (60 min). Adjacent to our [architecture-with-AI rubric](../../people/interview-rubrics/architecture-with-ai.md). Live, with AI tooling available, on a real-shaped problem.
5. **Reference and final fit** (60 min). Two senior engineers from the company you'd partner with. Less interview, more "are we going to enjoy working together."

The total interview load is ~6 hours of real work plus screens. We do not run more than this, and we do not run a separate "behavioral round."

## What this role is NOT becoming

We don't think this role merges into engineering in 2026. The customer-relationship dimension is real and persistent. We do think the bar continues to rise on the engineering side; we'll keep the JD calibrated as it does.

If you join, your career path includes options to move into product engineering, customer-facing platform work, or staying in SE through a Principal SE level (we have one). See [`career-path-and-comp.md`](career-path-and-comp.md).

## Apply if

- You write production code today and miss writing it
- You enjoy customer-facing work but resent demo-only roles
- You can articulate why an AI-generated solution is wrong, with specifics
- You want to be evaluated on what you ship, not what you can explain

## Don't apply if

- You haven't written production code in 3+ years
- Your engineering depth is bootcamp-level or self-taught without verification
- You're looking for a path to sales (this isn't it)
- You hate writing
