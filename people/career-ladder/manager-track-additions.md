# Manager Track Additions

Additions to your existing engineering manager career ladder. Maps to Chapter 60 of [_Software Engineering with AI_](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ryanbyrd) by Ryan Byrd.

The manager track changes less than the IC track. The job is still hiring, calibrating, developing, retaining engineers and translating the team's work into business outcomes. What changes is the substance of those conversations.

---

## Three additions that apply across all manager levels

1. **AI-tooling fluency.** The manager understands the tools the team uses well enough to ask useful questions. Not daily-driver fluency; conceptual fluency.
2. **The new performance review structure.** Manager runs the three new sections (harness contribution, AI authorship attribution, review discipline) competently. See [`../perf-reviews/`](../perf-reviews/).
3. **Retention play readiness.** Manager has the data, the framing, and the comp authority to retain a senior engineer who has become market-portable. See Ch 60 §60.4.

---

## M1 — Engineering Manager (first-level)

### AI-tooling fluency
- Understands what skills, hooks, subagents are conceptually. Can read a SKILL.md and ask a useful question.
- Knows the team's harness well enough to articulate what makes the team's work distinctive.

### Performance review and 1:1s
- Runs the three new perf review sections competently. Distinguishes "the engineer shipped a working feature" from "the engineer shipped a working feature AND contributed to harness."
- In 1:1s, asks about the engineer's recent agent-assisted work — what surprised them, what worked, what they'd do differently.

### Retention
- Tracks which of their engineers have become market-portable. Has had explicit conversations about career goals and comp expectations.
- Knows the team's six-metric dashboard (Ch 50) and can use it in conversations with leadership.

### Promotion to M2 requires
- Has retained at least one market-portable senior engineer through a recruiting attempt. The retention play worked because it was prepared, not improvised.
- Has shipped at least one cross-functional change (with PM, Design, or Customer Success) that incorporated AI-tooling considerations into the cross-team workflow.

## M2 — Senior Engineering Manager

### AI-tooling fluency
- Strong conceptual model of where AI tooling helps and where it doesn't. Can explain the team's productivity patterns to a non-technical executive.
- Partners with platform-team leadership on the team's harness needs. Knows what to ask for and what not to.

### Performance review and team development
- Has graduated multiple engineers through the new IC ladder additions. Track record of promoting on harness contribution + review judgment + Direction/Architecture/Evaluation depth.
- Coaches first-level managers on the new performance review structure.

### Cross-functional and executive
- Translates AI-tooling investment into business outcomes for leadership. Six-metric dashboard fluency. Comfortable defending the harness team's headcount.
- Holds the productivity-plateau conversation honestly (Ch 60 §60.2 — the conversation, not the technology, is the hard part).

### Promotion to M3 requires
- Has shaped one or more cross-team practices that the broader org adopted: a hiring rubric, a review practice, a retro pattern, a calibration process.
- Has retained a senior bench through a market cycle, not just a one-off.

---

## M3 — Director / Senior Director

### AI-tooling fluency
- Architectural understanding of the org's harness investment. Decides what to centralize vs. distribute.
- Owns the conversation with the CFO about AI-tooling spend. Can defend the budget without overpromising returns.

### Org design
- Designs reporting structures that account for the new bottleneck disciplines. Decides whether to specialize Direction / Architecture / Evaluation or keep them blended.
- Sets the bar for the new perf review structure across multiple teams. Calibrates managers under them.

### Hiring and retention
- Owns the senior bench retention strategy. Has the comp authority and the political capital to renegotiate bands when the market moves.
- Sets the org's hiring update cadence: when does the JD get refreshed, when does the interview rubric change, when does the interviewer training run.

---

## What this rubric will NOT do

- Will not turn a non-technical manager into a technically credible one. AI-tooling fluency at M1 is a floor; a manager who hasn't written code in 8 years and has no curiosity about what's changed will struggle.
- Will not work in a culture that doesn't value engineering management as a craft. The new manager track requires the org to take EM craft seriously; if EM is a default landing pad for senior engineers who don't know what else to do, no rubric will save it.

## What we explicitly DO NOT do

- **No separate "AI EM" track.** Same reasoning as the IC track: it ghettoizes the role.
- **No "AI Director" title.** The seniority comes from the work, not the buzzword.
