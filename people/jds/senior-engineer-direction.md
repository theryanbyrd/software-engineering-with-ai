# Senior Software Engineer (Direction)

**JD template** for an engineer with depth in the Direction discipline — deciding what to build and what "good" feels like when it's built. From Ch 5 §5.2.

This is one of three discipline-specific senior templates (Direction / Architecture / Evaluation). Use this template if your team needs a specialist in Direction; use the AI-native generalist template if you don't yet need to specialize.

---

## About the role

[REPLACE — 2-3 sentences about the team]

We're hiring a Senior Engineer with depth in **Direction**: the discipline of deciding what is worth building and what good feels like when it's built — in detail and within the realm of feasibility. You'll be the engineer who turns ambiguous product asks into vivid user stories, walkthrough-able experiences, and crisp specifications that an agent can execute against.

This is not a Product Manager role with a different title. You write code. You ship features. You also do the Direction work that, on most teams, gets pushed downstream into engineering as the back-and-forth that the handbook calls "GIGO — garbage spec, garbage delivery."

## What you'll do [KEEP]

- Translate ambiguous asks into specifications detailed enough that an AI agent can execute on them with high first-pass quality.
- Ship the resulting features end-to-end. The Direction work is one third of the job; engineering execution is the other two thirds.
- Write the user stories, the walkthroughs of the experience under normal use AND under failure, the definitions of what the user will trust the system to do unattended, the failure-mode catalog.
- Run the inverted-brief pattern (Appendix J) by default before significant agent work. Coach the team to do the same.
- Partner with PM, Design, and Customer Success to keep the team's understanding of "what good feels like" current.
- Review specifications written by other engineers; raise the floor on spec quality across the team.

## What we're looking for [KEEP]

**Required:** Senior IC AI-native baseline (see [`senior-engineer-ai-native.md`](senior-engineer-ai-native.md)) PLUS:

- Demonstrated specification clarity. You can show us 2-3 specifications you've written that produced clean first-pass agent work.
- Customer-facing or customer-adjacent experience. Direct conversations with users, support ticket triage time, sales-call shadowing — anything that has put you close enough to the customer to know what they actually do.
- Comfort with ambiguity. The Direction discipline starts when "make it good" is the requirement.
- Strong written communication. Direction work IS writing; if your prose isn't sharp, the agent's output won't be either.

**Preferred:**

- Background in user research, product management, design, or technical writing — any of which would have built the customer-empathy muscle.
- Experience working on product-leading teams where Direction was a named discipline.

## Interview process [KEEP]

1. Intro call (30m).
2. **Spec-writing exercise** (90 minutes async). We give you an ambiguous product ask plus customer context, and ask you to produce a specification an agent could execute on. We grade on the spec, not on whether you can also write the code (we'll trust the AI-native baseline interview for that).
3. PR review exercise (60 minutes async).
4. Architecture-with-AI conversation (60 minutes).
5. Direction conversation (60 minutes): walk through a recent feature you specified and shipped. What was ambiguous? How did you resolve it? What did you get wrong?
6. Team interviews (3 × 45m).
7. References.

## What we won't do [KEEP]

- We won't ask you to invent product strategy. That's the PM's job. We're hiring for the engineering-shaped version of Direction, not for product leadership.
