# Engineering Manager — AI-Native Team

**JD template** for the engineering manager of an AI-native team. This role is the most operationally-changed of any in the rollout: the work is different, the metrics are different, the conversations with the team are different.

## About the role

[REPLACE — 2-3 sentences about the team and product]

We're hiring an Engineering Manager to lead a small AI-native team (5-8 engineers). You'll own the team's roadmap, the team's harness contributions, the team's productivity metrics, and the conversations — with each engineer, with leadership, with finance — that translate AI-native engineering into business outcomes.

This is a hands-off role on day-to-day code, but you must be deeply technically credible. The team will not respect a manager who can't read a diff or ask a useful question about a skill design.

## What you'll do [KEEP]

- Lead a team of 5-8 engineers. Hire, calibrate, develop, retain.
- Own the team's roadmap and the conversation with the rest of the org about what the team is working on and why.
- Translate the team's AI-tooling investment into business outcomes for leadership: throughput, quality, cost, retention. Use the six-metric executive dashboard (Ch 50 §50.x) or your team's equivalent.
- Coach individual engineers on the new craft: when to delegate to an agent, when to write the code yourself, how to know the difference. The senior IC role description on this team is what you should expect from your engineers; coach toward it.
- Partner with the platform team on harness needs. Surface the team's skill, subagent, and hook requests. Push back when the platform team is pulled in too many directions.
- Run weekly retros that include the four-bucket triage (Score / Question / Opportunity / Train) on the week's failed agent attempts (Ch 19 §19.5, Ch 31 §31.5).
- Have honest conversations about expectations. When the team's productivity gain plateaus, you have the data and the framing to renegotiate from. When a senior engineer is at risk of leaving for the market premium, you have the retention play ready.

## What we're looking for [KEEP]

**Required:**

- 3+ years of engineering management experience (or 5+ as a tech lead with significant people responsibilities)
- Technical credibility. We don't expect you to be the strongest IC on the team, but you should be able to read a PR, understand an architecture diagram, and ask useful questions about a skill design.
- Familiarity with AI engineering tooling. You don't need to be a daily user, but you should have used the team's stack enough to understand the work.
- Experience translating engineering metrics into business outcomes. You can write a one-page memo to a CFO that explains why throughput plateaued.
- Comfort with the new performance review structure: harness contribution, AI authorship attribution, review discipline (Ch 60 §60.3).

**Preferred:**

- Direct experience leading a team through an AI tooling rollout, especially at the mid-size scale (50-200 engineers in the org).
- Background in either the senior IC role on an AI-native team OR a strong platform background. Either gives you the credibility this role requires.
- Comfort with operational practices: incident response, on-call, cost telemetry. AI tooling rollouts increase ops complexity; you should not be allergic.

**Things that are NOT requirements:**

- A specific people-management framework certification.
- Hands-on AI tooling at the level of a senior IC. You'll need fluency, not depth.
- Algorithm-style technical interviews on your past experience. We hire EMs on judgment, not on whiteboard problems.

## Interview process [KEEP]

1. **30-minute intro call.**
2. **Management scenario discussion** (60 minutes). We share three real (anonymized) scenarios — a productivity plateau conversation, a senior engineer at risk, a budget-cap-from-finance moment — and discuss how you'd handle them.
3. **Coaching round** (45 minutes). One of our senior ICs walks through a real performance situation; you coach them.
4. **Cross-functional partner round** (45 minutes). With Product, Design, or Customer Success — depending on the team's primary partners.
5. **Skip-level conversation** (45 minutes). With the engineering org's leader.
6. **Reference checks.**

## What we won't do [KEEP]

- We won't ask you to do a system design as if you're an IC. The signal we want is your judgment about teams, not architecture.
- We won't pressure-test you with "if you don't take this job today" tactics. The handbook calls these out specifically (Ch 56 §56.x); we don't use them.

## For the recruiter / hiring manager

The hardest signal to assess in this role is whether the candidate has the technical credibility to be respected by an AI-native team. A candidate who has been managing for 8 years but stopped reading code in 2020 will struggle. A candidate with strong recent engineering background but limited management experience can often grow into the role faster than the reverse.

If your candidate funnel skews toward "career managers," consider widening to include strong tech leads who are ready for their first official manager role.
