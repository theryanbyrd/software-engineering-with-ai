## The discipline

Per Ch 49 §49.4 of the handbook:

- Read **one substantive piece per week**.
- Discuss **one piece per month** with the platform team.
- Write **one short note per quarter** on what changed in your understanding.
- The reading list is not the goal. The goal is a calibrated set of expectations built from primary sources, not vendor keynotes.

## Why a dated list

Reading lists go stale faster than any other content in this domain. The people writing the most useful work in mid-2026 may not be the same in mid-2027. Each entry has a `dated_through` field; entries past that date fall off the active list automatically during quarterly review. This protects against the canon ossifying around writers who have stopped producing useful work.

## Contributing

See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) for the editorial bar, the entry format, and what gets accepted vs. declined. The short version:

- Every entry must have a specific reason it earns a slot. "It's popular" is not a reason.
- No vendor-pitching content. No "thought leadership" that's a marketing piece in disguise.
- Submit by editing `data.json` and opening a PR; the maintainer renders the README during quarterly review or accepts inline as appropriate.

## Retirement criteria

Entries are retired (moved to `_retired` in `data.json`) when:

- The URL is dead and there's no replacement.
- The author has left the field or stopped publishing.
- The content is now stale (e.g., a benchmark that's been gamed, a vendor blog that's gone marketing-only).
- A better alternative has emerged that covers the same ground more sharply.

Retirement is normal and expected. A reading list that doesn't retire entries becomes inaccurate within 12-18 months.
